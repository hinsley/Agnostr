Solar map credits

These maps are fixed visualizations, not live weather or survey data. From a distance, Venus and Titan show their cloud tops. Their detail maps reveal the ground beneath the haze.

Planets, Earth and the Moon

Mercury, Venus (surface and atmosphere), Earth as seen from space, the Moon, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune: Solar System Scope, CC BY 4.0. Agnostr resized, compressed and packed these maps for display. The source uses NASA imagery, adjusted colors and representative terrain where data is missing. Cloud bands and storms are illustrative.

Focused Earth keeps its separate, zoomable topographic basemap from Esri World Topographic Map and contributors, including OpenStreetMap contributors.

Focused Moon imagery and zoom tiles use the NASA GSFC Scientific Visualization Studio LROC globe mosaic (Ernie Wright; NASA Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter). It is resampled and compressed into tiles up to 8192 × 4096 equivalent. Polar coverage uses lower-resolution laser albedo measurements. The distant overview uses the Solar System Scope map at the same longitude and latitude registration. Its orientation uses NASA/JPL’s Mean Earth/polar-axis model to show the near side from Earth, including date-dependent lunar libration.

Pluto and the outer moons

Base colors from NASA Visualization Technology Applications and Development (VTAD): Pluto, Charon, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Enceladus, Rhea, Titan (haze), Titania, Oberon and Triton. Only the cylindrical color images were extracted and downsampled; no original models are shipped. Smooth or blank areas, notably on Titania, Oberon, Charon and Triton, can reflect missing observations rather than featureless terrain.

Titan beneath the clouds

Cassini ISS global map, PIA19658. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech/Space Science Institute. These near-infrared observations show surface brightness through the atmosphere. Agnostr cropped the frame, shifted the longitude origin and tinted the grayscale signal amber for readability; this is not a visible-light photograph.
